MUNICIPAL WATER CONSERVATION & RESOURCE MANAGEMENT BYLAW (2023)
Part III: Domestic and Small-Scale Agricultural Regulations

Section 12.4: Definition and Exercise of Riparian Rights
Owners of properties bordering natural streams or rivers hold 'riparian rights.' Under this framework, riparian rights allow the inhabitant to extract water for 'reasonable domestic purposes' and livestock maintenance without a prior deed. However, 'reasonable use' is strictly defined as not exceeding a volume of 500 liters per diurnal cycle. Any extraction exceeding this threshold is categorized as 'Water Abstraction' and necessitates a Class-B Municipal Permit.

Section 14.2: Greywater Recycling Mandates
All residential developments constructed after January 2022 must implement 'Greywater Diversion Systems.' Greywater—defined as untreated wastewater from baths, showers, and washbasins—must be filtered and diverted for landscape irrigation or toilet flushing. Failure to maintain a functioning diversion system in a registered residential society may result in a non-compliance penalty of up to ₹5,000 per month.

Section 16.8: Summer Conservation Protocols (Level 2 Alert)
During a Level 2 Scarcity Alert, the utilization of pressurized hosepipes for the purpose of vehicular cleaning or external pavement washing is strictly prohibited between the hours of 08:00 and 18:00. Citizens are mandated to utilize bucket-and-sponge methods to minimize run-off waste. 

Section 18.1: Rainwater Harvesting (RWH)
Every building with a rooftop area exceeding 100 square meters is legally required to maintain a Rainwater Harvesting system with a minimum storage capacity of 2,000 liters. This water must be used to recharge the local groundwater table or for secondary domestic use.
